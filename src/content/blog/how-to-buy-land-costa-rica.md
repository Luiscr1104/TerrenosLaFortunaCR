Buying land in Costa Rica is a dream for many, but it requires careful navigation of the legal and procedural landscape. Whether you're looking for a retirement haven, an investment property, or a vacation home, understanding the process is crucial to a successful purchase.

## 1. The Right to Own Property

Foreigners have the same rights as Costa Ricans when it comes to purchasing and owning property. You can own property in your own name or through a corporation. The only exception is concession land within the Maritime Zone (first 200 meters from the high tide line), which has specific restrictions.

## 2. Due Diligence is Key

Before signing anything, thorough due diligence is essential. This includes:

*   **Title Search:** Verifying the property's legal status in the National Registry.
*   **Cadastral Plan:** Ensuring the survey map matches the property boundaries.
*   **Zoning and Land Use:** Checking local regulations (Plan Regulador) to confirm you can build what you intend.
*   **Utilities:** Verifying access to water and electricity.
*   **Taxes:** Ensuring all property taxes are paid up to date.

## 3. Personal Name vs. Corporation

You can buy property in your personal name or through a Costa Rican corporation (Sociedad Anónima or Sociedad de Responsabilidad Limitada). Using a corporation can offer benefits for estate planning and liability protection, but it also involves setup and maintenance costs.

## 4. The Purchase Process

1.  **Offer to Purchase:** A formal offer outlining the price and terms.
2.  **Sale and Purchase Agreement:** A detailed contract signed by both parties, usually accompanied by a deposit held in escrow.
3.  **Closing:** The final transfer of the deed (Escritura de Traspaso) before a Notary Public.

## 5. Closing Costs

Closing costs typically range from 3.5% to 5% of the purchase price and are usually shared between buyer and seller, though this is negotiable. These costs cover transfer taxes, legal fees, and registration fees.

## Conclusion

Investing in Costa Rica is a rewarding experience when done correctly. Working with a reputable real estate agent and a knowledgeable attorney is the best way to ensure a smooth and secure transaction.
